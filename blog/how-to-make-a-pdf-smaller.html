2
Convert to Grayscale High impact
Colour images store three channels of data (red, green, blue) per pixel. Grayscale stores one. Converting a colour PDF to black and white reduces image data by roughly two thirds — without any visible quality loss for text documents, invoices and reports where colour isn't meaningful.
Typical reduction: 40–70%

Use RightPDFKit's Grayscale PDF tool — then compress the result for maximum reduction.

3
Remove Unnecessary Pages Targeted
If your PDF has blank pages, appendices, cover pages the recipient doesn't need or sections that don't apply — removing them directly reduces size proportionally. A 30-page PDF with 10 removable pages becomes a 20-page PDF. No compression needed, no quality loss.
Reduction: proportional to pages removed

4
Split Into Smaller Files When size limit is the issue
If the PDF genuinely needs all its content but is too large to send in one go — split it into sections. A 25MB report becomes two 12MB files, each within most email limits. The recipient gets the same content, just in two attachments.
Effective for: email limits, upload caps

5
Compress + Grayscale (Combined) Maximum reduction
For scanned documents, invoices, forms and any PDF where colour isn't essential — convert to grayscale first, then compress the result. This two-step approach achieves the largest possible reduction. A 20MB scanned report can realistically reach 1–2MB using both steps.
Typical reduction: 80–95%

Use Grayscale PDF first, then run the result through Compress PDF on High or Maximum.

6
Re-export From the Source If you have the original
If you created the PDF from Word, PowerPoint or another application — re-export it with lower quality settings rather than running it through a compressor. "Save As PDF" settings in Word include quality options. Adobe Distiller and print-to-PDF both offer resolution controls. This often produces smaller files than post-processing compression.
Best for: PDFs you created yourself

In Word: File → Save As → PDF Options → reduce image resolution to 150 DPI for email quality.

7
Use a File Transfer Service Instead When quality must be preserved
If your PDF contains high-quality images that genuinely need to stay sharp — architectural drawings, photography portfolios, print-ready files — don't compress them. Use WeTransfer, Google Drive or Dropbox to share the full-quality file via a link instead of an attachment.
Best for: design files, print-ready PDFs

Right-click the file in Google Drive → Get link → Share with recipient.

Why your PDF won't compress further

If running a PDF through a compressor produces minimal reduction, it's usually because:

  • Already been compressed — images are already at a low quality setting. Compressing again produces tiny additional gains and visible quality loss.
  • Text-only document — PDFs with only text and vector graphics have very little image data to compress. A 5MB text document compresses to around 4.5MB at best.
  • Already optimised at export — PDFs exported from Google Docs, modern Word versions and some PDF printers apply their own optimisation on creation.

In these cases, the split-and-send or file-transfer-link approaches are the practical solutions.

Frequently asked questions

How do I make a PDF smaller to email?

Open RightPDFKit, click Compress PDF, upload your file, choose High compression and download. If the result is still too large, also convert to grayscale first using the Grayscale PDF tool, then compress the result. Both tools are free and process locally — no upload required.

Why is my PDF so large even with no images?

Embedded fonts are the most common culprit for large text-only PDFs. Some fonts, particularly decorative or custom fonts, embed their full font file into the PDF. The Compress PDF tool strips redundant font data while keeping the text readable.

Can I make a PDF smaller without losing quality?

Yes — at Low compression levels, size reduction is achieved by removing metadata and optimising file structure rather than downsampling images. The visual quality difference is imperceptible. Quality only degrades at High and Maximum compression settings.

Why won't my PDF compress any smaller?

The PDF has likely already been compressed — either on creation or by a previous compression pass. At that point, images are already at minimum viable quality and further compression produces minimal gains. Try splitting the PDF into sections or sharing via a file transfer link instead.
