Best Free PDF Editor in 2026 — Tested and Ranked

Most "best free PDF editor" lists recommend the same tools — Adobe, iLovePDF, Smallpdf, PDF24 — without mentioning the detail that matters most in 2026: which ones upload your files to a server?

For a marketing brochure, that doesn't matter. For a contract, payslip, medical letter or anything with personal information — it does. This guide covers the six most capable free PDF editors, tested honestly, with a clear answer on what each does with your data.

2. iLovePDF — Best known, but uploads everything

iLovePDF is one of the most visited PDF tool sites in the world. The interface is clean, the tools work reliably, and the free tier covers the core tasks. The catch: every file you process is uploaded to iLovePDF's servers in Spain, processed, and then deleted after a period.

Free tier: Most tools are available but with file size limits (typically 15MB) and daily task limits. Merge and compress are the most commonly hit.

Paid: iLovePDF Premium removes limits and adds batch processing. Pricing starts at around £4/month.

Privacy: Files are uploaded to their servers. Their privacy policy states files are deleted after processing, but you have no way to verify this.

Best for: Non-sensitive documents where convenience matters more than privacy. Fast, reliable, no installation.

3. Smallpdf — Polished, limited free tier

Smallpdf has one of the most polished interfaces of any online PDF tool. It handles compression particularly well — the output quality at small file sizes is excellent. The free tier, however, is very restricted: two tasks per day, with a hard limit that resets at midnight.

Free tier: Two tasks per day. No file size limit. Clean output with no watermark.

Paid: Smallpdf Pro starts at around £9/month. Includes unlimited tasks, batch processing, and e-signing.

Privacy: Files are uploaded to Smallpdf's servers. GDPR compliant, files deleted after an hour.

Best for: Occasional use where the 2-task daily limit isn't a constraint. Best compression quality of the online tools tested.

4. PDF24 Creator — Best free desktop option (Windows only)

PDF24 Creator is a free Windows desktop application. It covers the core PDF tasks — merge, split, compress, rotate, convert — without any limits, watermarks or uploads. Everything runs locally on your machine.

Free tier: Fully free with no limits. No account required. No internet connection needed.

Limitations: Windows only. No mobile support. Interface is functional but dated. No AI features. Doesn't run in the browser — requires installation.

Privacy: Local processing. No uploads.

Best for: Windows users who need a reliable offline PDF tool and don't want to use a browser-based tool.

5. LibreOffice Draw — Best for true text editing (desktop)

LibreOffice Draw is the most capable free PDF text editor available. Open a PDF and each page loads as an editable canvas — you can click on text blocks, change them, move elements, add images and export back as PDF. It's the closest free alternative to Adobe Acrobat's editing capabilities.

Free tier: Fully free, open source, no limits. Available on Windows, Mac and Linux.

Limitations: Complex PDFs sometimes lose formatting on import. No browser version. Requires a 300MB+ download. Interface is not intuitive for PDF-specific workflows.

Privacy: Fully local. No internet required.

Best for: Power users who need real text editing and don't mind installing desktop software.

6. Adobe Acrobat — Industry standard, expensive

Adobe Acrobat is the reference standard for PDF editing. The feature set is unmatched — tracked changes, advanced redaction, Bates numbering, portfolio PDFs, advanced form creation. For professional document workflows, nothing comes close.

Free tier: Adobe Reader (view and comment only). Acrobat online tools are available but with strict limits. True editing requires Acrobat Standard (£12.99/month) or Acrobat Pro (£18.99/month).

Privacy: Online tools upload files to Adobe's servers. The desktop application processes locally.

Best for: Professional workflows where editing power matters more than cost. Legal, publishing, enterprise.

The upload question — why it matters in 2026

Most people don't think about this until something goes wrong. But consider what passes through PDF tools in a typical week: a signed contract, a payslip, a bank statement, a medical letter, an NDA. Every time you upload one of these to iLovePDF, Smallpdf or Adobe's online tools, a copy exists on their server — however briefly.

Under GDPR, UK data protection law and many NDAs, uploading personal data or confidential information to a third-party server is a data handling decision that should be made consciously, not accidentally.

Local processing tools — RightPDFKit in the browser, PDF24 Creator on Windows, LibreOffice on desktop — eliminate this risk entirely. The file never leaves the device.
