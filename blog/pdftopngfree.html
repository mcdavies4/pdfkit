Why PNG and not JPG?

PNG is a lossless format — it preserves every pixel exactly. This makes it ideal for documents with text, diagrams, logos, and sharp edges. JPG compresses the image and can introduce blurring around text. If your PDF contains photos only and file size matters, JPG is fine. For everything else, PNG gives you a cleaner result.

When should you convert PDF to PNG?

  • Presentations — insert a PDF page as an image into PowerPoint or Google Slides
  • Social media — share a flyer, invoice, or infographic as an image post
  • Websites — embed a document preview without requiring a PDF viewer
  • Messaging apps — send a quick screenshot of a document page via WhatsApp or Slack
  • Archiving — save individual pages as images for easy browsing on mobile

What about image quality and resolution?

RightPDFKit renders each PDF page at high resolution (typically 150–300 DPI), so text stays crisp and images stay sharp. The output PNG files are full-quality — no watermarks, no downscaling, no compression artifacts. If your original PDF is high-quality, your PNG will be too.

For scanned PDFs that look blurry, the issue is the scan quality, not the conversion. You can try running OCR first to extract the text, then work with the extracted content directly.

Is it safe to convert PDFs online?

With most tools (iLovePDF, Smallpdf, Adobe), your file is uploaded to a remote server for processing. If your document contains financial data, personal information, or business-sensitive content, that's a real risk. RightPDFKit processes everything locally in your browser using WebAssembly — your file stays on your device from start to finish. Nothing is transmitted, nothing is stored.
