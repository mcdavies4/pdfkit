2. LibreOffice Draw — Best free desktop alternative

LibreOffice Draw Best desktop option
libreoffice.org · Free · No account · Desktop (Windows, Mac, Linux)
LibreOffice Draw is the most powerful free desktop PDF editor available. It lets you edit text, move images, add shapes and annotations, and export back to PDF — all locally without uploading anything. It's not as polished as Acrobat but it's free, open source and doesn't require an internet connection.
    ✓ Pros
  • Fully offline — no internet needed
  • Open source and free forever
  • True PDF text and image editing
  • Available on Windows, Mac and Linux
    ✗ Cons
  • Requires download and install (~350MB)
  • Interface dated compared to modern tools
  • No merge, compress or convert tools built in
  • Can't do OCR or sign PDFs natively

3. PDF24 — Best free unlimited cloud option

PDF24 Best unlimited cloud option

PDF24 is the most generous cloud-based PDF editor — no account required, no daily limits, and a wide range of tools. Files are uploaded to their servers for processing and deleted after use. Their free desktop app (Windows only) processes files locally. A solid pick for non-sensitive documents.
    ✓ Pros
  • No account, no daily task limits
  • 25+ tools including OCR and conversion
  • Desktop app for local processing (Windows)
  • PDF24 Chat — AI chat with PDF feature
    ✗ Cons
  • Files uploaded to servers in browser version

7. Adobe Acrobat Reader — Free but view-only

Adobe Acrobat Reader Free — view and sign only
acrobat.adobe.com · Free · Adobe account required · Desktop + mobile
Adobe Acrobat Reader is the original — free to download and excellent for viewing, printing and signing PDFs. But it's view-only. You can't edit text, merge, compress or convert without upgrading to Acrobat Standard (£12.99/month) or Acrobat Pro (£17.99/month). Worth having for viewing PDFs, but not a real alternative for editing.
    ✓ Pros
  • Best-in-class PDF viewing experience
  • Free to use for viewing and signing
  • Available on all platforms
    ✗ Cons
  • No editing, merging or converting for free
  • Adobe account required
  • Heavy install (~600MB desktop)
  • Nags about upgrading to Pro constantly

What Adobe Acrobat Pro does that free tools can't

To be fair to Adobe — there are things Acrobat Pro genuinely does that free alternatives don't match:

  • Certified digital signatures — legally binding with a cryptographic audit trail, accepted in enterprise and legal settings
  • Advanced PDF form creation — build interactive PDF forms from scratch with conditional logic
  • Deep Microsoft 365 integration — edit PDFs directly inside Word, Excel and Teams
  • Cloud document storage — share, collaborate and track document activity across teams
  • Advanced redaction — scrub metadata and hidden text as well as visible content
  • Accessibility tools — compliance with WCAG and PDF/UA standards

For personal use and most small business workflows, none of these matter. For legal teams, enterprise procurement or regulated industries — Adobe's advantages are real.
