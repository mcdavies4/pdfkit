What the certificate page includes

The certificate page is what makes RightPDFKit's eSign different from a simple signature image:

  • Signer name, email, job title, organisation
  • Reason for signing (Approval, Reviewed, Certified, Witnessed)
  • Exact timestamp in UTC
  • Pages signed
  • SHA-256 hash of the original document — a unique fingerprint of the PDF before signing. If the document is altered after signing, the hash won't match. This is verifiable proof of document integrity.

Is an electronic signature legally valid?

In the UK, eIDAS Regulation and the Electronic Communications Act 2000 recognise electronic signatures as legally valid. In Nigeria, the Evidence Act and Cybercrimes Act provide a framework for electronic signatures. In the US, the ESIGN Act covers electronic signatures.

For most commercial contracts, NDAs, and business agreements, an electronic signature with a timestamp is legally sufficient. For high-value transactions or court filings, consult a lawyer in your jurisdiction.

Frequently asked questions

Is the signature legally binding?

Electronic signatures are legally recognised in the UK, EU, Nigeria, and US for most commercial agreements. The certificate page provides timestamp and integrity proof.

What is a SHA-256 hash?

A SHA-256 hash is a unique digital fingerprint of your document. If anyone changes even one character after signing, the hash changes — proving tampering. It's the same technology used in blockchain and digital certificates.

Can the other party verify the signature?

Yes — the certificate page includes all verification details. Anyone can hash the original document and compare it to the hash on the certificate.

Does my signature get stored anywhere?

No — your signature is drawn in your browser, applied to the PDF locally, and the result downloads to your device. Nothing is stored or transmitted.
